Where the three shutoffs live on most heaters

Finding them in daylight saves a scramble later.

1

Water

Look for a lever or round handle on the cold pipe entering the top. If it will not turn, the main shutoff at the street side of the house works instead.

2

Gas

A quarter-turn valve sits on the line just before the heater's gas control. When the handle lies across the pipe, the gas is off.

3

Power

Electric heaters have a dedicated double breaker, and some have a service disconnect switch on the wall within sight of the unit.

Repair or replace, how to tell a dead tank from a bad part

Replacement is right when the tank wall itself leaks, when sediment has hardened across the bottom, or when the unit has already needed several repairs. It is wrong when a thermostat, an element or a valve has failed on an otherwise sound tank. A plumber who sells you a new heater without testing the old one is skipping the diagnosis.

Your answers to a few questions help. Is the water coming from the tank body or a fitting, has the hot water been brown, and is the heater far past the warranty on its label? Our water heater repair page covers what can be saved.

What a same-day swap looks like from drain-down to first hot tap

Most tank swaps take part of a day, and the order matters.

  1. Drain and disconnectThe plumber closes the supply, drains the tank through a garden hose to a safe spot, then disconnects the water, gas or power and the vent.
  2. Remove the old unitA failed tank full of sediment is heavy, and stairs or attics call for a second set of hands.
  3. Set the new heaterIt sits in a drain pan where code and layout call for one, on a level base, with the drain valve clear.
  4. Connect and protectNew flexible connectors, a dielectric union or nipple pair, a relief valve with a proper discharge line, and an expansion tank where the system is closed.
  5. Vent, fill and testOn a gas unit the plumber checks draft at the vent, leak-tests the gas joints, purges air from the lines and confirms the burner lights.

Venting, relief valves and expansion tanks the quote should mention

A gas heater needs a vent that pulls combustion gases out of the house and enough air to burn. A new heater with a different draft or flue size may not suit the old vent, and the plumber adjusts or replaces it. This is where a cheap quote hides trouble.

The temperature and pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe that runs downhill to a visible, safe end. An expansion tank soaks up the extra volume of heated water when a pressure reducing valve or backflow device closes the system, and the plumber can test the house pressure to see whether one is needed.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Building and Safety Division at City Hall whether your swap needs a permit and an inspection. The California Plumbing Code in Part 5 of Title 24 governs the work, and the 2025 edition took effect January 1, 2026.

Warranty, haul-away and the questions worth asking any installer

Ask what the tank warranty covers and for how long, and whether labor is part of it. Ask whether removal and disposal of the old tank is included, since not every quote includes it. Ask whether the connectors, the relief valve and the discharge line are new or reused.

Ask to see the contractor's license. The Contractors State License Board lists a C-36 plumbing classification, and anyone can look a license up on the CSLB Check A License page. Planning ahead instead? Water heater installation covers the checklist for a scheduled job.

What drives the price of a replacement heater

We quote a clear price by phone and again on site before work starts.

  • Tank size and type. A 50 gallon unit costs more than a 40, and a heat pump model costs more than either.
  • Fuel and conversion. Changing from electric to gas or the reverse means new lines or circuits.
  • Venting and gas work. A new flue, a larger vent or an upgraded gas line adds labor.
  • Code items. A drain pan, an expansion tank and new relief piping each add parts.
  • Access and haul-away. An attic or a tight closet is slower, and disposal may or may not be included.
  • Hour of the call. Overnight and weekend visits cost more than a scheduled one.

Standard homeowner insurance usually covers a sudden burst and not a tank that wore out, so check your policy. Call the dispatcher for the real number.

How long does a tank swap take?

Many take part of a day from drain-down to the first hot tap, depending on access and any vent or gas work.

Does a new heater come with haul-away?

Will my warranty cover the labor?

Manufacturer warranties often cover the tank or parts and not labor, but terms differ by model. Read the card that comes with the unit.

Can I switch from tank to tankless during an emergency swap?

Sometimes. A tankless unit needs the right gas line, vent and wall space, and it may not be in stock the same day.

Many people install a tank now and plan the conversion for later.

What affects the cost of replacing a water heater?

Size, fuel, venting, code items, access and the hour of the call all play a role.
