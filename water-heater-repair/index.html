Five checks that sort out a heater with no hot water

These take a few minutes and give the dispatcher something useful to work with. Skip any step that involves removing a cover on a powered electric heater.

  1. Look at the floorA puddle or a wet drain pan means a leak, and the next section applies. A dry floor points to a heating failure.
  2. Check the flame or igniterOn a gas unit, look through the viewing window at the bottom. A pilot that will not stay lit usually means a worn thermocouple or a sooty pilot tube.
  3. Find the breaker and the reset buttonAn electric heater runs on a double breaker. Behind the upper thermostat cover sits a red reset button for the high-limit switch, and a tripped one is worth a call before you press it again.
  4. Compare the dial to the tapA setpoint turned down to a low mark or a vacation mode looks like a failure but is not one.
  5. Time how long the hot water lastsIf it runs out within minutes while the heater is working, a cracked dip tube may be sending cold inlet water straight to the outlet.

Water around the base of the tank and what can still be saved

Not every puddle means a dead tank. A weeping drain valve, a loose supply connector, a dripping temperature and pressure relief valve or condensation on a cold pipe can all leave water on the floor, and each one is a small repair. The plumber dries the area, wraps tissue around each fitting and watches where it darkens.

A leak from the tank body is another story. Once the steel has rusted through behind the insulation, no patch holds, and the water keeps coming until the tank is drained. If the plumber confirms that, the honest answer is a swap, and our water heater replacement page explains how that goes.

A heater of any age that has leaked and flooded a drain pan should not be left under pressure overnight. Close the supply, open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure, and wait for the plumber.

Gas burner, electric element or hybrid heat pump, what tends to fail

The failure list depends on the fuel, so the first question on the phone is always what the heater runs on.

Gas tank

Thermocouples, flame sensors, gas control valves and clogged burner ports cause most no-heat calls. A pilot that blows out in wind or backdrafts may point to a venting problem instead.

Electric tank

Upper and lower heating elements burn out, thermostats drift, and the high-limit switch trips. A multimeter on each part tells which one failed without guessing.

Hybrid heat pump unit

These have a compressor, a fan, an air filter and condensate drain on top of the usual parts. A clogged filter or a blocked condensate line is a common reason for a fault code.

Tankless unit

Scale, flow sensors and igniters are the usual suspects, and they follow their own rules. See tankless water heater service for that side.

Popping, rust-colored water and a dripping relief valve

Smaller symptoms often show up weeks before a full failure.

  • Popping or rumbling. Sediment on the tank floor traps steam bubbles under it. A flush clears light buildup, while a thick crust can leave the heater inefficient for good.
  • Rusty or brown hot water. If only the hot side is discolored, the anode rod is likely spent and the tank wall is corroding. Replace the rod early and the tank can last longer.
  • Pilot keeps going out. Check for drafts, then expect a thermocouple or a gas control valve to be next.
  • Relief valve dripping. This may be real overpressure from a failed expansion tank or a valve that is worn. Never cap the valve, because it is the last safety on the heater.

Ask the plumber to show you the old part. It confirms what failed.

Heaters in garages and attics during a 99 F July

Shafter's July highs average about 99 F, and an attic or a closed garage runs hotter than the outside air. That heat dries out gaskets, cooks plastic fittings and makes an undersized closet struggle to vent. Keep boxes and chemicals at least a few feet from the burner, and keep the vent clear.

Cold matters less here, since hard freezes are rare, but a record low of 14 F is a reminder to insulate exposed pipes on the coldest nights. We also take repair calls from McFarland, roughly 15 miles away, so say your town when you phone.

What changes the cost of a water heater repair

We quote a clear price by phone and again on site before work starts. These factors move it.

  • The failed part. A thermocouple is a smaller job than a gas control valve or a heat pump component.
  • Fuel and design. Gas, electric, power-vent and hybrid units each need different parts and tools.
  • Access. A heater in a tight closet or an attic takes longer to work around than one in an open garage.
  • Tank condition. Heavy sediment or corroded fittings can turn a quick fix into a longer visit.
  • Hour of the call. Overnight and weekend visits cost more than a scheduled weekday appointment.

Standard homeowner insurance usually covers sudden damage and not wear, so check your policy before assuming a failed heater is covered. Call the dispatcher for the real number on your unit.

Water heater repair questions from Shafter homeowners

Do you handle water heater repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Shafter?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including overnight and weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Tell the dispatcher what the heater runs on and whether there is water on the floor. That decides what the plumber brings.

Can I relight the pilot myself?

Many gas heaters print the steps on a label near the control. Follow it exactly, and stop if you smell gas or if the pilot goes out again within minutes.

A repeat outage means a part is failing, and relighting only hides it for a day.

Is it safe to keep using a heater that drips from the relief valve?

A short drip right after a heating cycle can be thermal expansion. A steady drip is a warning that the valve is worn or pressure is too high.

Do not plug the valve. Turn the heater to its lowest setting and call.

Why is my hot water brown or smelly?

Rust color on the hot side only points to the tank or the anode rod. A rotten egg smell often comes from bacteria reacting with the rod.

A flush and a new rod fix some tanks. Others are too corroded to bother.

Is my leaking tank repairable?

If the water comes from a valve or a fitting, yes. If it comes from the tank wall or the base seam, no.

The plumber dries the tank and watches where water returns, then tells you which case it is before any work.

What affects the cost of a water heater repair?

The failed part, the fuel type, access to the heater, the condition of the tank and the hour of the call all play a role.

Call for the clear price before work starts. If the tank is near the end, you will hear that too.
