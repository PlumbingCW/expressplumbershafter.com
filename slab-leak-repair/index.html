A slab leak suspected tonight and what to do first

Emergency slab leak repair in Shafter, CA is the work of finding and fixing a pressurized water line that has failed in or beneath the concrete foundation of a house. Our dispatcher takes calls 24/7. Until a plumber arrives, close the main shutoff and set the water heater to its off or vacation setting so it stops feeding hot water into a damaged line.

Do not chip, drill or saw the floor to look for the leak. Lift rugs, move electronics and boxes off the floor, and stay off any spot that is hot to the touch, because that means heated water is moving right beneath it. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a midnight call reaches the same dispatcher. Call (855) 575-2890 and say where the warm patch is.

Clues that point down into the concrete

No single sign proves a slab leak, but a few together make it very likely.

A floor that is warm in one place

A hot water line running through or under the slab heats the surface above it. Bare feet notice before any gauge does.

Running water sound with everything off

A faint hiss near a wall or a closet floor, strongest in a silent house at night, can be a pressurized leak.

A meter that will not stop

If the indicator turns after the main shutoff at the house is closed, the loss is outside, and if it stops, the leak sits on the house side.

Water heater running constantly

A hot line draining into the ground makes the heater refill and reheat all day, and the gas or electric use climbs.

Lifting tile, swollen baseboards or damp carpet edges

Water forced upward finds the weakest seam. Grout lines darken and floor coverings loosen.

Pressure that has dropped overall

A steady loss under the foundation can pull pressure down at every fixture.

How the leak is confirmed before anyone cuts concrete

The order matters, because each step narrows what the next one has to search.

  1. Meter and shutoff checkWith all fixtures off, the plumber watches the low-flow indicator and repeats the test with the house main closed.
  2. Pressure test by lineHot and cold supplies are capped separately. A line that will not hold pressure is the guilty one.
  3. Electronic listeningA sensitive microphone is walked across the floor on a grid. The hiss grows loudest right above the break.
  4. Thermal imagingA hot line leak shows as a warm bloom on the floor. This also separates a hot line from a cold one.
  5. Marking and explainingThe spot is marked with tape, the route of the pipe is traced, and the plumber walks you through the options.

Why foundation leaks stay hidden for so long

Slab foundations are common in this part of the Central Valley, and on a slab house the supply lines often run through or under the concrete. A pinhole there has no place to drip, so the water soaks the gravel and soil below and may show up weeks later as a damp edge or a higher bill. The hidden path is the real problem.

Copper wears where it rubs against rough concrete or a sharp fitting, and hot lines expand and contract every day, which loosens the sleeve around them. Acidic soil and aggressive water can eat at a pipe from outside and inside. Not every leak under a house is on the supply side. A cracked drain line leaves a sewer smell and a wet slab edge, and a sewer camera inspection is the tool for that.

Three ways to repair a leak beneath the foundation

The right choice depends on where the pipe runs, how many leaks it has, and how much floor you are willing to lose.

Spot repair through the slab

The plumber cuts a small window in the concrete directly over the marked leak, replaces a short length of pipe, and patches the opening. It suits a single leak on an otherwise healthy line. The trade-off is dust, noise and a patch that may show through tile or wood flooring.

Reroute the line overhead

A new line is run through the attic or wall cavity to the fixtures, and the old line under the slab is capped off. It avoids cutting the floor, and it is the better answer when the same pipe has failed more than once. It does mean opening some walls and patching drywall afterward.

Repipe the affected loop

When pipe throughout the house shows pinholes, a new set of lines may beat repeating spot repairs. Our repiping page explains the materials and the process, and it is a bigger job with a bigger bill than a single patch.

Flooring, drywall and what happens after the repair

Tile can usually be lifted and relaid if spare pieces exist, while glued wood and laminate rarely come back looking the same. Carpet is pulled back and dried, and wet padding is replaced. We handle the plumbing, and finish flooring, drywall and paint are separate trades. Ask the Building and Safety Division whether the job needs a permit, because permit rules vary.

What decides the cost of a slab leak fix

Prices vary by house, so these are the factors that move the number.

  • Repair path A spot repair, a reroute and a repipe are very different amounts of labor.
  • Pipe depth and floor covering Cutting through tile over thick concrete takes longer than through bare slab.
  • Number of leaks Finding one leak is common, but a line with several may be better replaced.
  • Access overhead Attic height, insulation and wall layout decide how hard a reroute is.
  • Drying and finish work Fans, dehumidifiers and patching can be separate costs from the plumbing.

Standard homeowner insurance usually covers sudden damage and often not the wear that caused the pipe to fail, so check your policy. Adjusters usually look for a written leak location report, moisture readings, photos and the date you noticed the problem. Call for the real number, and a clear price comes before work starts.

Slab leak questions from Shafter homeowners

Do you handle slab leak repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Shafter?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, overnight and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Close the main shutoff, turn the water heater to off or vacation, and keep the dispatcher on the line with what you see.

How do I know the warm spot is a leak and not the sun?

A sun-warmed floor cools along with the room and follows the window light. A leak spot stays warm at night and sits along the path of a hot line.

If the meter also turns with every fixture off, call.

Do you have to break the whole floor?

No. Listening gear and thermal imaging narrow the leak to a small area, and a spot repair opens a window in the concrete of modest size.

A reroute avoids the slab altogether by running a new line through the attic or walls.

Can a slab leak damage the foundation?

A long-running leak can wash out soil under the concrete and soften the ground that supports it. That is why a confirmed leak should not wait for months.

A structural engineer, not a plumber, judges whether movement has already happened.

Is a slab leak the same as a sewer leak under the house?

No. A slab leak is normally a pressurized water line. A sewer leak is a drain pipe that carries waste and holds water only while it runs.

Smell and a damp slab edge point at the drain, and a camera shows the break.

Will the leak show up on my bill?

Often, though not always. A small leak can add a little to every bill, while a large one makes the meter spin visibly.
