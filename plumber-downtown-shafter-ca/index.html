What counts as downtown Shafter

Downtown Shafter is the older core of the city, where homes and businesses sit close together. Shafter began as a loading dock on the Santa Fe Railroad right-of-way, and the Green Hotel, built in 1913 near Central Avenue and James Street, is still the oldest commercial building in town.

No public source gives a firm boundary for downtown, so this page uses the Central Avenue and James Street area as its anchor. If your street is a few blocks off, say so and name the nearest cross street. The downtown murals and the Green Hotel are easy landmarks to mention.

Age alone does not tell you what is behind the wall

Homes.com describes small one-story bungalows in town alongside newer ranch-style subdivisions. A neighborhood data site reports that most homes in the city center tract were built between 1970 and 1999, with a number from 1940 to 1969. Those are broad ranges from outside sources, not an inspection of any house.

A home's age does not tell you its pipe material. Copper, galvanized steel, cast iron and plastic can all turn up in houses of the same decade, because owners repair and replace over time. A look at an exposed pipe, a camera inspection of the drain or a pressure test tells you more than a build year.

Renting, owning and who to call first

About 40 percent of Shafter housing units were renter-occupied in the 2020 Census, so a good share of residents are tenants. If you rent, tell your landlord first, because repairs to pipes and fixtures are usually arranged through the owner.

Owners have a different first question, which is where the city's responsibility ends. The City of Shafter provides water, sewer and trash service, so ask City of Shafter utility services which part of a line the city maintains before you pay for a repair.

Sorting out a slow drain in an older house

These steps help separate a single clog from a main line problem.

  1. Count the drainsRun a sink, a tub and a toilet. One slow drain is a local clog, and several slow drains point toward the main.
  2. Skip the chemicalsDrain chemicals can damage fixtures and hurt the person who opens the line later. Try a plunger first.
  3. Find the cleanoutLook for a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard. Knowing where it is shortens the visit.
  4. Ask for the right toolDrain cleaning clears the clog, and a sewer camera inspection shows what the line looks like when drains keep returning.

Downtown Shafter plumbing questions

Where is downtown Shafter?

Downtown is the older core of the city, anchored by the Green Hotel near Central Avenue and James Street. No public source gives a firm boundary, so name your street and the nearest cross street when you call.

Does an older house mean old pipes?

Not by itself. Owners repair and replace pipes over time, so houses of the same age can have different materials. A look at an exposed pipe, a camera inspection or a pressure test tells you more than the year.

Who is responsible for a sewer clog, me or the city?

The City of Shafter provides sewer service, but which part of a line the city maintains is a question for City of Shafter utility services. Ask them before you pay for a repair, and tell the person who answers what you learned.

Does this page cover businesses as well as houses?

Downtown mixes both. Houses are covered by the residential service pages, and shops and restaurants by the commercial plumbing page. Describe the building when you call.

Do I need a permit for a repair?

That depends on the job. Ask the City of Shafter Building and Safety Division at City Hall whether a specific repair needs a permit, because permit rules vary. Plumbing work in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, Part 5 of Title 24.
