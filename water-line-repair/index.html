A line leaking right now and who is responsible for which stretch

Emergency water line repair in Shafter, CA covers the buried pressurized pipe that carries water from the meter to your house, and the dispatcher answers 24/7. If water is bubbling up in the yard or the street side of your house is soaked, close the main valve at the house and keep children, pets and mowers off the soft ground.

Leave the valve inside the meter box alone, because it belongs to the utility. Ask the City of Shafter utilities where the city side ends, since that decides who fixes a leak near the box. Meter, box, service line and house plumbing, one pipe at a time

Naming the stretch helps the dispatcher and the utility.

City side of the meter

The main under the street and the tap that feeds your meter. A leak here is for the utility, and the dispatcher can tell you to report it.

The meter box

A box full of water, a hissing fitting or a wet ring around the lid can mean a leaking connector on either side of the meter.

Service line from meter to house

The buried pipe on your side. This is the line this page is about, and its leaks are usually invisible until the ground softens.

House shutoff and plumbing

The valve where the line enters, plus the regulator in many homes. A failing valve or regulator can mimic a buried leak.

Symptoms of a failing line and what to do first

These signs often show up before water surfaces.

  • A wet or sunken patch Grass stays green, mushy or sinks in a stripe along the line.
  • A hissing noise Quiet at the faucet but audible at the hose bib or near the house wall.
  • Rising bill The meter indicator turns with every fixture off and the house main closed.
  • Muddy or discolored water A break can draw soil or sediment into the line when pressure drops.
  • Low or surging pressure A leaking line steals flow before it reaches the house.

Hidden leaks inside the house point to leak detection instead, and a split inside a wall is handled as a burst pipe.

Low water pressure at every fixture in the house

Low pressure at one faucet is usually a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure everywhere is a line, valve or regulator problem. Screw a pressure gauge onto a hose bib with everything else off. A steady reading in the 40 to 60 psi range is typical, and a reading above 80 is commonly considered hard on fixtures and supply hoses.

When the regulator is the culprit

A pressure regulator is a bell-shaped valve near the entry that holds the house at a steady pressure. When the internal diaphragm fails, pressure can creep up overnight or sag during use. A gauge reading that moves with no one running water points at the regulator, and replacing it is a short job.

When the street is the problem

If neighbors on the block report weak pressure, or the gauge falls when a nearby fire hydrant is used, the cause may lie on the city side. Contact the City of Shafter utilities, since a utility problem is not yours to repair.

Partial valves and old steel

A main valve that is only partly open throttles flow, and old galvanized steel narrows with rust until a full-size fixture gets a trickle. Replacing a short run or the whole service line restores flow.

Finding a buried line leak without trenching the whole yard

A pressure test on the line between the meter and the house tells us if it is losing water. A ground microphone and a correlator then listen along the pipe, and tracer gas can pin down a small hole. The goal is a mark on the lawn within a few feet, so the crew digs one pit and not a long ditch.

Before any digging, call 811, the Underground Service Alert number, so that buried gas, electric and utility lines are marked. This is a free, standard step, and any excavation for a leak depends on it. Soil in an irrigated yard can move water a long way, so do not trust the wet spot alone.

The choice depends on the yard, the pipe and what sits on top of it.

Old steel lines, new service lines and permits

Galvanized service lines wear out from the inside, and a leak often means the rest of the pipe is thin as well. Replacing the whole line is usually wiser than repairing it twice. If the house plumbing is also steel, our repiping page covers what happens inside the walls.

New lines are needed when a detached shop, a garage bathroom or a new meter location is added, and a plumbing installation crew can tie them in with a shutoff valve. Ask the Building and Safety Division whether the job needs a permit and an inspection, because permit rules vary.

What changes the price of a water line job

The number depends on the dig, and these are the real factors.

  • Length and depth A short run is quicker than a long one buried below the frost-free depth.
  • Surface on top Concrete, pavers and planted beds cost more to restore than bare dirt.
  • Method Spot repair, trench and pull-through need different crews and equipment.
  • Pipe material Copper, PEX and HDPE differ in cost and in what the yard allows.
  • Utility coordination Locates and any work at the meter box may add steps and time.

Standard homeowner insurance usually covers sudden damage and often not a line that wore out on its own, so check your policy. Call for the real number, and a clear price comes before work starts.
