The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

When you need an emergency plumber in Shafter, CA, the first minutes decide how much damage a leak does. Find the main shutoff, usually a gate valve or ball valve near the front hose bib or at the meter, and turn it clockwise until it stops. Then open a low faucet to drain what is left in the pipes.

If a water heater is leaking, close its cold supply valve and cut the breaker or gas control. If a toilet or drain is overflowing, stop all water use in the house. Then Call (855) 575-2890, and the 24/7 dispatcher takes your address and the symptoms.

Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a full line, and do not wait until morning on a burst pipe. A shutoff valve that will not turn is worth telling the dispatcher about, since the plumber brings different tools for a stuck valve.

The problems that fill the phone

Blocked drains are the most common call. A cable machine clears a sink, tub or floor drain, and emergency drain cleaning covers that work. When every drain in the house runs slow, the main sewer line is the suspect, and sewer line cleaning uses a larger machine at the cleanout.

Grease and scale that a cable only punches through call for a jetter, which is what hydro jetting means in practice. A camera visit through sewer inspection shows whether a pipe is cracked or sagging before anyone digs.

No hot water is the next big category, handled by water heater repair, or by replacement when the tank has failed. Water in places it should not be goes to leak detection, and a pipe that has split open is a burst pipe repair job.

Smaller fixes matter too. A running toilet, a dripping valve or a jammed disposal gets a visit through toilet repair, faucet repair or garbage disposal repair. Businesses with after-hours trouble can use commercial plumbing dispatch.

Checking a plumber's license before you let them in

The Contractors State License Board licenses California contractors, and C-36 is the Plumbing Contractor classification. Anyone can open the Check A License page at cslb.ca.gov and look up a contractor by name or license number. Ask any plumber, including ours, for that number and check it.

California plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The 2025 edition took effect on January 1, 2026. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Building and Safety Division at City Hall whether yours needs one.

If you smell gas, leave first

Gas line work is not a service we sell. If you smell gas, leave the building at once, then from a safe place call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 if SoCalGas is your provider, as your bill shows. Call 911 if anyone could be in danger or you cannot reach the utility. We also do not do electrical, HVAC, roofing or pool equipment repair.
