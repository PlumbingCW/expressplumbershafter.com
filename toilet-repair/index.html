An overflowing toilet right now

Emergency toilet repair in Shafter, CA covers an overflowing, leaking or broken toilet that a licensed plumber fixes by replacing parts, clearing the trap or resetting the bowl. Our dispatcher takes calls 24/7. If water is rising, take the tank lid off, lift the float or flapper so the tank stops refilling, and close the angle stop on the wall behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.

Do not flush again to test it. Lay towels along the base and keep the bathroom fan on. Call (855) 575-2890 if the stop will not turn, because forcing a seized valve can snap the stem and turn a small spill into a leak.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Overnight and weekend calls go to the same dispatcher, and the plumber who comes out carries flappers, fill valves, supply lines and wax rings.

A toilet that runs all night, part by part

Most running toilets fail in the tank, in the order below. Check them from the cheapest part to the most work.

  1. FlapperDrop a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl without flushing, the rubber flapper is leaking.
  2. ChainA chain that is too tight holds the flapper up. Too loose and it tangles. A little slack, about the width of a pencil, is right.
  3. Fill valveIf water keeps hissing and runs into the overflow tube, the fill valve or float setting is off, or a worn seal has let go.
  4. Water levelThe level should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. Higher than that wastes water with every refill.
  5. Flush valve seatA pitted or scaled seat will not seal even with a new flapper. A replacement flush valve fixes it.

Rocking, weeping at the base and the wax ring

A wax ring seals the toilet outlet to the flange, which is the ring on the floor that the drain pipe ends in. Water around the base after a flush, or a toilet that shifts when you sit, means the seal has failed or the closet bolts are loose. A new ring is cheap, but the reason it failed matters.

The flange may be cracked, rusted or sitting too low after new tile went down. A raised ring or a repair flange brings it back to the finished floor. On a slab foundation, floor tile also hides a leak well, so a soft or stained spot near the toilet should be checked before you reset anything.

Do not caulk the base all the way around the bowl. A small gap at the back lets a slow leak show itself.

Plunger, closet auger or pull the toilet

A flange-style plunger is the right tool for most bowl clogs. Push slowly, then pull, and keep the seal tight for about a dozen strokes. If water drops and returns after each flush, the trap holds a toy, a wipe or too much paper.

A closet auger reaches the trap bend without scratching the porcelain. When it does not work, or when the tub and floor drain gurgle as well, the clog sits in the line behind the toilet and calls for a drain cleaning visit. Chemical cleaners stay out of the picture, because they can eat the wax ring and burn skin.

We pull the toilet only when an object is lodged in the trap. A slow flush with a clean bowl often means blocked rim jets or a partial clog, not a bad toilet.

Supply line, angle stop and a puddle that will not stop

A damp spot on the wall side of the tank, or a drip from the connection under the tank, comes from the supply line or the tank bolts. Braided stainless lines last longer than cheap plastic ones. A seized or weeping angle stop should be swapped, not tightened with a wrench, since a leaky stem packing gets worse the harder you crank.

If the water shuts off and the leak stops, the problem is in the stop or the line. If a puddle keeps growing after the stop is closed, a pipe in the wall or floor may be leaking, and a plumber should trace it. The same visit can cover a dripping sink, which our faucet repair page explains. We also take toilet calls in McFarland, roughly 15 miles away.

Repair the toilet you have or replace it

Parts fix most toilets. The table shows when a new bowl makes more sense.

Rocks or leaks at the baseNew wax ring, bolts or flangeBowl base is cracked or the flange is badly broken

Constant cloggingAuger and a camera on the linePoorly designed trap and a clean line behind it

Ask the plumber for both numbers when the toilet is old. A repair that keeps an old unit running is fine, but not if it fails again next month.

Setting a new toilet, rough-in and height

Before you pick a toilet, measure the rough-in, which is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the floor drain. 12 inches is the most common and a 10 or 14 inch rough-in needs a matching model. Comfort height seats sit a little taller and are easier on the knees.

A newer low-flow toilet uses less water per flush, and many are designed to clear the bowl in a single pass. If you want more fixtures or a layout change, our plumbing installation service handles that work. Ask the Building and Safety Division whether a new toilet or a moved drain needs a permit, because rules vary.

What changes the cost of a toilet visit

We quote a clear price before work starts. These factors move it.

  • The part. A flapper is small, a flush valve or fill valve is more, and a full toilet is most.
  • The floor. Tile or slab may need careful lifting, and a damaged subfloor adds work.
  • The flange. A cracked or low flange needs its own repair.
  • The clog. A bowl clog is quick. A clog past the toilet needs a cable.
  • The hour. Overnight and weekend calls cost more than scheduled weekday visits.

Standard homeowner insurance usually covers sudden damage and not wear or a slow leak, so check your policy. Call for the real number.

How do I stop a toilet from overflowing?

Lift the flapper or float so the tank stops refilling, then close the angle stop on the wall. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

If the stop will not turn, close the main shutoff to the house and call.

Why does my toilet rock a little?

The closet bolts may be loose, or the flange may sit below the floor tile. A rocking toilet breaks its wax seal sooner or later.

Do not just tighten the bolts hard, since porcelain cracks.

Is a running toilet worth fixing?

Yes. A flapper that leaks can waste water every hour of the day and raise your bill. It is one of the cheapest repairs there is.

If the tank cracks or the seat is pitted, a new toilet may cost less over time.

Can I use a drain cleaner on a clogged toilet?

Not a good idea. The liquid sits in the bowl, can splash back and harm skin, and it may weaken the wax ring.

A flange plunger or a closet auger works better and does not hurt the porcelain.

What does toilet repair cost?

It depends on the part, the floor, the flange, whether a clog is involved and the hour of the call.
