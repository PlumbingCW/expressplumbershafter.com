A burst pipe or flooded restroom at your business

Emergency commercial plumbing in Shafter, CA means a licensed plumber responds to a failed pipe, drain, restroom or water heater at a shop, warehouse, restaurant or farm building. The dispatch line runs 24/7, so a Friday night flood gets the same answer as a Tuesday morning leak. Before anyone arrives, find the main water shutoff and close it, then move stock and paper off the floor.

If the shutoff is stuck or you cannot find it, tell the dispatcher. Keep staff away from standing water near outlets and equipment. Call (855) 575-2890 and say how many restrooms, kitchens or drains are affected.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and weekend or after-hours calls reach the same dispatcher. Every hour with a closed restroom or a dead kitchen line is lost sales, so we start by stopping the water and restoring what can be restored before the full repair is quoted.

Finding a leak in a commercial building

Commercial leak detection is the call we get most, and honesty matters here. Our plumber can test a building with a meter check, isolate zones by closing valves one at a time, and use listening gear and thermal imaging to narrow a spot. Some leaks still need a wall, ceiling tile or slab section opened to confirm.

We do not trace leaks inside large process piping or fire sprinkler systems, and no tool promises to find every leak. If the zone test shows loss on one branch, we tell you which branch and what it would take to reach it. Read how leak detection methods work before you approve any cutting.

Backflow assemblies and who can test them

A backflow assembly sits on a water line to keep used or dirty water from being drawn back into the supply. Many commercial properties have one on the irrigation, the fire line or the main. The water provider can require an annual test, so check with them for your property.

Ask us what we can test and what needs a certified tester. We repair or replace leaking assemblies, relief valves and the shutoffs around them. If the assembly drips from its relief port, report it, because the cause can be a worn check, not a nuisance.

Grease, floor drains and storm water

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the sewer. When it fills, kitchen drains slow, smell and back up into the prep area. Cleaning the trap and then jetting the line it feeds keeps grease from coating the pipe, and hydro jetting does that scouring.

Floor drains in a shop or warehouse collect silt and oil. They overflow in the worst possible spot, right where forklifts and wheeled carts travel.

Storm drains and yard grates

Storm drain cleaning clears leaves, silt and trash from catch basins and the pipe beyond them. Rain in Shafter averages about 7.3 inches a year, mostly November to March, so a clogged grate can sit unnoticed all summer and flood the lot on the first big storm. We cable or jet the line and run a camera when it keeps filling.

Restrooms, water heaters and tenant improvements

Commercial restrooms take far heavier use than a house. Flushometer valves wear, supply stops seize and a toilet that runs all day can add a lot to the water bill. A plumber will check the stops, the flange and the drain line, and we handle repairs like those on a toilet repair call as well.

Water heaters in shops and break rooms fail the same way house tanks do, with a leaking tank or a dead element or burner. For tenant improvements, we run new supply and drain lines for sinks, mop basins and restrooms to a standard hookup, and a permit may be involved, so ask the Building and Safety Division. Our crews serve Wasco businesses too, roughly 10 miles up State Route 43.

What we do not do is large industrial process piping or fire sprinkler systems. Those need contractors with that specialty.

What moves a commercial plumbing quote

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the real drivers.

  • Size of the building. Long pipe runs take longer to isolate and test.
  • Access. Ceilings, mechanical rooms and slab floors change the labor.
  • Parts availability. Valves, assemblies and large tanks may need to be ordered.
  • Business hours. Work during opening hours costs more because of the way it must be scheduled.
  • After-hours rate. Overnight and weekend calls cost more than a weekday visit.
  • Permits or testing. Some work needs a permit or a certified tester.

Commercial property insurance often covers sudden damage and not wear, so check your policy and your lease for who pays for what. Call the dispatcher for the real number.
