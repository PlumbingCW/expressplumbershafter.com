From Deweyville to the crossing of two state routes

Rose rows, nurseries and the pipes that water them

Wasco is known for growing roses. In 2007 about 55 percent of the roses grown in the United States came from the Wasco area, and about 40 percent by 2011. Properties that sit next to nursery or field work often have long irrigation runs, extra hose bibs and a yard sprinkler line off the house supply.

Those outdoor lines are where many leaks start. A hose bib that drips behind a bush can run for weeks before a bill shows it, and a cracked riser to a sprinkler valve can soak a foundation edge.

Spot a wet strip along the wall or a soft patch in the yard? Our leak detection service uses a meter test, a listening device and a tracer gas or thermal tool to locate the pipe before anyone digs.

Hot dry summers and the water heater closet

Wasco has a semi-arid climate with very hot, dry summers and mild to cool winters, and the mean annual temperature is about 64 F. A water heater in a garage, attic or hall closet cooks in that summer air. Heat ages the relief valve, the thermostat and the gaskets on the elements faster than most owners expect.

The usual calls are a heater with no hot water, a pilot that will not stay lit, a tank that is weeping at the bottom, or a pressure relief valve that is dripping. Our water heater repair plumbers carry thermocouples, thermostats, heating elements and relief valves on the truck. A tank that leaks from the shell, not a fitting, is usually past repair.

If you are standing in water beside the heater, turn off the cold supply valve on top of the tank and cut the power at the breaker or the gas control. Do not touch the unit with wet hands. For a failed tank, water heater replacement is the next conversation.

Drains in houses, shops and equipment yards

A kitchen line that fills with grease, a tub that holds water or a laundry standpipe that gurgles are the most common calls we get from the Wasco side. The plumber starts with a P-trap and a cable machine, then moves to a jetter if the line holds scale or roots.

Wasco also has oil and gas extraction in its history, with the Wasco Oil Field discovered in 1938. Shops and yards that serve field work have restrooms, floor drains and wash sinks that clog like any other. When that happens after hours at a business, our commercial plumbing dispatch handles it the same way as a home call.

If the main line itself is backing up, every drain in the building will slow together. That points to a stoppage beyond the last branch, and a cleanout cap near the foundation is where the plumber will start. Read more about emergency drain cleaning before the plumber arrives. Drain work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so a cleanout is placed where the code expects it.

Before We Arrive at your Wasco address

These five steps take a few minutes and make the visit shorter.

1

Find the main shutoff

On most Wasco lots it sits near the street side hose bib or in a box by the meter. Tell the dispatcher if you cannot turn it, because a stuck valve changes what the plumber brings.

2

Cut power near water

Switch off the breaker for a flooded water heater closet or a garage outlet that is wet. Stay out of standing water until power is off.

3

Clear the way

Move cars from the driveway edge, open the gate and put the dog inside. A rural lane with a blocked gate slows the whole visit.

4

Stop using the drains

Do not run the dishwasher, washer or shower if a drain is backing up. Every gallon you add has to come out somewhere.

5

Note what you see

Take a phone photo of the wet area, the heater label and the meter. Then call the 24/7 line and read the dispatcher your street and cross street.
